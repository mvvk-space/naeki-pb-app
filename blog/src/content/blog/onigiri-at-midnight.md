Grab-and-go has an honesty problem: the food was made earlier, and everyone
knows it. The last hour of a kiosk's day is where that honesty is tested.

## The rotation is the product

A rotating featured menu isn't merchandising — it's a freshness contract. If the
salmon-ikura mayo onigiri is today's featured, it was pressed this morning, and
the batch that didn't sell is tomorrow's lesson about how many to press.

- Featured items turn over by day-part, not by week.
- Sashimi-grade cuts move first; cooked fillings hold the tail of the day.
- Anything past its window leaves the case, not the price tag.

## Why this is in the app

The showcase models this on purpose: featured rotation, branch availability, and
the loyalty milestones are all seeded from the same data a real counter would
run on. The journal is where the *why* lives; the app is where the *what* does.

Same brand, two jobs.
